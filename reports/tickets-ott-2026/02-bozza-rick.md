Hi Rick,

Thank you for this, and I'm sorry it took a few days — and sorry you hit these at all. Your report was unusually good: the PDF, the response body and the extraction ID let me reproduce all three bugs in minutes. All three are fixed, verified against your own `factura.pdf`. Here is each one, plus a straight answer on OCR that you are not going to like.

**1. The vendor name**

You were right, and the cause is embarrassing. Our supplier-name matcher had the Spanish word `de` in its list of labels, and it wasn't anchored to the start of a line. On your `Fecha de facturación: 2026-10-27`, it matched the preposition and took the rest of the line as the company name. Any Spanish invoice with a `Fecha de …` line hit this.

Labels are now only recognised where they actually read as labels — followed by a colon, or opening a line. I also added the Spanish labels that were simply missing: `Proveedor`, `Cliente`, `CIF`/`NIF`. Worth knowing: `Cliente` could never match before, because our pattern looked for `client` with a word boundary and the trailing `e` defeated it. That is why your customer block came back empty.

On your file, `vendor.name` now reads `Empresa Ejemplo`.

**2. The due date it invented**

Also our bug, and the worse of the two. When no due-date label was found, the engine fell back to returning the first date it saw anywhere in the document. On your invoice that was the issue date, so `invoice_date` and `due_date` came back identical — you can see it in the response you sent me, both `2026-10-27`.

A due date now requires an actual label (`Vencimiento`, `Fecha de vencimiento`, `Due date`, …). When the document doesn't state one, the field is empty. On your file `due_date` is now `""`. Nothing is inferred from payment terms — we would rather return nothing than a date you might pay against.

While I was in there I added Spanish month names, so `15 de marzo de 2026` is now read correctly too.

**3. Spanish status, English labels**

Our bug, and the fix is live. The envelope builder was looking up a property that doesn't exist on any of our result types, so it ended up labelling its own plumbing — that's why you saw `"data": "data"` and `"metadata": "metadata"` instead of field names. With `lang=es` you now get:

```json
"localized": {
  "lang": "es",
  "status": "Extraído",
  "labels": {
    "invoiceNumber": "Número de factura",
    "invoiceDate": "Fecha de emisión",
    "dueDate": "Fecha de vencimiento",
    "vendor": "Proveedor",
    "customer": "Cliente",
    "total": "Total"
  }
}
```

One correction on usage, and this one is our documentation's fault, not yours: the parameter is **`lang`** (or `output_lang`). `language` is not a request parameter — it is a _response_ field reporting the document language we detected. Our docs listed it in the request table, so you were following them exactly. We're correcting that page.

The extracted values in `data` keep their stable English machine keys by design, so your Activepieces mapping won't move. `localized.labels` sits alongside, for display.

**4. OCR — the honest answer**

The DealFuel listing is wrong, and it is wrong because we wrote it.

ParseFlow has no OCR engine. Not disabled, not gated behind a plan, not partially implemented — there is no OCR code in the product and never has been. Images are rejected with HTTP 422 on every tier, and a scanned PDF with no text layer fails the same way. Our documentation is the accurate one; the listing bullet promising "OCR for scanned documents" came from marketing copy we supplied to the marketplace, and I've since corrected our copy and asked them to update the listing.

I'm sorry. You paid for a lifetime plan with that bullet in front of you, and that shouldn't have happened.

What ParseFlow does: documents that already contain selectable text — PDFs generated by software, DOCX, XLSX. If your Spanish invoices arrive as scans, the practical path is an OCR step upstream that outputs a searchable PDF, then ParseFlow for the structured fields. OCR is not on our roadmap, and I'd rather tell you that than leave it vague.

If OCR was a reason you bought, tell me and I'll refund you in full, no argument and no forms — you keep whatever use you've had of it.

**One thing that will look like a regression and isn't**

Confidence on your invoice now reads 0.80 instead of 0.85. The old score added points for any field that wasn't empty, so the wrong vendor name and the invented due date were both _raising_ it. It now only counts fields we actually read next to their own label. A lower, honest number is more useful to you than a high one on data we guessed — especially if you're gating anything in Activepieces on that value.

Your exact test case is now part of our regression suite, so it can't come back quietly.
